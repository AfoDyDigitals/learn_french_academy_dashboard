Which exam is right for you?

All four use the CEFR scale (A1 to C2). They differ in purpose, validity and scoring.

Rule of thumb. Applying to Canada or for French residency? Take the TCF or TEF; check which one your program accepts. Want a diploma you keep forever? Take the DELF or DALF at the level you're confident you can pass.

Works for every exam

Strategies by skill

Listening

  • Read the questions first. Use every second before the audio starts to underline who, what, when and why.
  • Predict. Guess the likely vocabulary before you hear it (a train station: quai, retard, billet).
  • Listen for signal words: mais, pourtant, en revanche often come right before the correct answer.
  • Beware of word traps. The option that repeats a word you heard is often wrong; the right one usually paraphrases.
  • If you hear it once only (TCF, TEF), answer immediately and move on.

Reading

  • Skim, then scan. 30 seconds for the general idea, then hunt for the line that answers each question.
  • Questions usually follow the order of the text.
  • Watch the details: negatives (ne… plus, ne… jamais), numbers, dates and conditions change the meaning.
  • Justify with the text. In the DELF, copy the exact phrase that proves your answer.
  • Never leave a multiple-choice blank. The TCF and TEF don't take points off for wrong answers.

Writing

  • Plan for 3–5 minutes: purpose, reader, register (tu or vous), three ideas.
  • Answer every part of the task. Missing a requested point costs more than a grammar slip.
  • Respect the word count. Too short loses points; count your lines as you go.
  • Use linking words (d'abord, ensuite, cependant, donc, enfin) and paragraphs.
  • Keep 5 minutes to reread: agreements, verb endings, accents.

Speaking

  • Use your preparation time to write key words only, never full sentences to read out.
  • Structure out loud: introduce, give 2–3 points with examples, conclude.
  • Keep talking. If you lose a word, describe it: c'est une chose qui sert à…
  • Ask the examiner to repeat if needed: Pourriez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?
  • Show range: one past tense, one future, one conditional and a few connectors.
Time management. Before the exam, write down the minutes you can spend on each part. During the exam, glance at the clock at every section change, not only at the end.

France Éducation international

TCF Canada

The most common version for our students. TCF tout public, TCF Québec and TCF IRN follow the same idea with some differences in length and tasks.

TestFormatTimeScore
Listening39 multiple-choice questions, 4 options, getting harder as you go35 min/699
Reading39 multiple-choice questions, 4 options60 min/699
Writing3 tasks: short message (60–120 words), article or letter (120–150), argued text from two documents (120–180)60 min/20
SpeakingFace to face: guided interview (~2 min), role play with 2 min to prepare (~5.5 min), giving your opinion (~4.5 min)12 min/20

Target for Canada: NCLC 7

Express Entry's French-speaker category asks for NCLC 7 in all four skills. Minimums: 458 listening, 453 reading and 10/20 in writing and speaking. Aim for a safety margin of about 480 / 475 / 12.

Listening and reading

  • Questions go from A1 to C2. Win the easy points early, but don't rush them.
  • Each listening extract is played once. Answer, then let it go.
  • Wrong answers aren't penalized: always pick an option.
  • In reading, pace yourself at about 90 seconds per question.

Writing and speaking

  • Task 3 (writing): summarize both documents' views neutrally, then give your opinion with reasons.
  • Speaking task 2: ask at least 6 varied questions (price, time, place, conditions).
  • Speaking task 3: state your opinion in the first sentence, then give 2 arguments and an example each.
  • Use vous with the examiner unless told otherwise.

CCI Paris Île-de-France

TEF Canada

Four tests taken on the same day, about 2 hours 55 minutes in total. Scores run from 0 to 699 per test and convert to CEFR and NCLC levels. The citizenship version keeps only listening and speaking.

TestFormatTime
Reading40 questions60 min
Listening40 questions40 min
WritingSection A: continue a news article (80+ words, 25 min). Section B: give and defend your opinion (200+ words, 35 min)60 min
SpeakingSection A: call to get information (5 min). Section B: convince a friend (10 min)15 min

Writing

  • Section A: keep the article's tone and facts; answer what, who, where, when; use past tenses correctly.
  • Section B: state your position, give at least 3 arguments with examples, and answer one counter-argument.
  • Word minimums are strict. Plan 80 and 200 words, not "about".

Speaking

  • Section A: aim for 10 or more different questions in 5 minutes. Vary the forms: est-ce que, inversion, quel/quelle.
  • Section B: the examiner will disagree on purpose. Stay polite, rephrase, and add a new argument each time.
  • Keep a list of ready phrases: Je comprends ton point de vue, mais…

Other TEF versions

TEF IRN (for French residency and naturalisation) is shorter: about 1 hour 30, scored 0 to 499, levels A1 to B2. Naturalisation applications from 1 January 2026 require B2 in both oral and written French, with a certificate under two years old.

Official help. CCI Paris publishes free expert tips for each TEF test. Find them in your dashboard library under TEF/TCF.

Diploma for life

DELF A1 to B2

Each level is a separate diploma. Listening, reading and writing are taken together on paper; speaking is a one-on-one interview, often on another day.

LevelWritten tests (together)SpeakingSpeaking prep
A1About 1 h 205–7 minAbout 10 min
A2About 1 h 406–8 minAbout 10 min
B1About 1 h 45About 15 minAbout 10 min
B2About 2 h 30About 20 minAbout 30 min
How to pass. Each of the 4 tests is marked out of 25, for a total of 100. You need 50/100 overall and at least 5/25 in every test. A very weak skill can fail you even with a good total.

A1–A2

  • Writing: fill in forms, write short messages and postcards. Check you included every requested detail.
  • Speaking: introduce yourself, answer questions about you, then a short role play (buying, booking, asking).
  • Learn numbers, dates, prices and times perfectly: they appear in every listening test.

B1–B2

  • Writing: a letter, article or essay with a clear opinion. At B2, a structured argument with examples is expected.
  • Speaking B1: interview, role play, then presenting your point of view on a short text.
  • Speaking B2: defend an opinion from a short article, then debate with the examiner. Introduction, 2–3 arguments, conclusion.

Practice with the real thing

France Éducation international publishes free official sample papers for every level. Do at least two full papers under timed conditions before your exam. Links are in your dashboard under Practice.

Advanced diploma

DALF C1

Same pass rules as the DELF (50/100 and at least 5/25 per test), with longer, more academic tasks. Written tests last about 4 hours in total.

TestFormatTime
ListeningOne long recording (heard twice) and several short radio clips (heard once)About 40 min
ReadingOne long text (about 1,500–2,000 words) with a questionnaire50 min
WritingA synthesis of 2–3 documents (about 220 words), then an argumentative essay (about 250 words)2 h 30
SpeakingA presentation built from 2–3 documents, then a discussion with the jury30 min +
1 h prep

The synthesis

  • Find the common theme, then group the documents' ideas by point, not by document.
  • Stay neutral: no personal opinion, no examples of your own.
  • Reuse key words, never whole sentences. Give it a title.
  • Stay close to 220 words; count them.

Essay and presentation

  • Essay: clear thesis, two or three argued parts, a nuanced conclusion. Match the requested format (letter, article, opinion piece).
  • Presentation: open with a hook and the problem (problématique), announce your plan, keep to about 10 minutes.
  • In the discussion, defend your view calmly and accept good counter-points.
DALF C2. The C2 is a single long written and oral exam on a chosen field. Only take it after a strong C1 result; ask your tutor for a dedicated plan.

Six weeks before

  • Book your exam and check the accepted version (TCF Canada, TEF Canada, DELF level…)
  • Do one full official sample paper to find your weakest skill
  • Agree a weekly plan with your LFA tutor

The final week

  • One timed listening and reading set every day
  • Two practice orals with your tutor
  • Review your error list: agreements, accents, verb endings
  • Check the exam address, start time and route

On the day

  • Valid photo ID that matches your registration
  • Convocation letter, black pens, a watch if allowed
  • Arrive 30 minutes early
  • Read every instruction twice before you start
